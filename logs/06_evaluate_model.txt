Part 6: Model Evaluation

Goal:
The goal of this step is to evaluate the baseline Logistic Regression model and understand how well it predicted customer churn. Since churn prediction is a business classification problem, I looked past accuracy and also reviewed precision, recall, F1-score, and the confusion matrix.

Steps:
1. Used the trained Logistic Regression model to make predictions on the test set.
2. Calculated the model's accuracy.
3. Generated a confusion matrix to compare actual churn values against predicted churn values.
4. Generated a classification report to review precision, recall, and F1-score for both classes.
5. Created a confusion matrix heatmap to make the results easier to interpret visually.

Results:
The baseline Logistic Regression model achieved about 80.7% accuracy.

The confusion matrix showed:
- 925 customers were correctly predicted as not churned.
- 110 customers were incorrectly predicted as churned.
- 162 customers were incorrectly predicted as not churned even though they actually churned.
- 212 customers were correctly predicted as churned.

The classification report showed that the model performed better on customers who did not churn than customers who did churn. For the churn class, the model had 66% precision, 57% recall, and a 61% F1-score.

Interpretation:
Accuracy alone is not enough for this project because most customers did not churn. A model could get a decent accuracy score by mostly predicting “no churn.” Since the business goal is to identify customers who may leave, recall for the churn class is especially important.

The model caught 57% of the customers who actually churned, but it missed 162 churned customers. This means the baseline model is useful, but there is room for improvement.

Conclusion:
The Logistic Regression model is a successful baseline model. It performs reasonably well overall, but future improvements should focus on increasing recall for churned customers so the company can identify more at-risk customers before they leave.
